# Checkpoint 2 — notes (Thu 15 Oct 2026, reviewer: COO)

*Live demo of Harbour Bank, then the reviewer drove Coral Pay. About 25 minutes. Summarised by Claude from Lina's transcript; quotes are the reviewer's words, lightly cleaned.*

## What the reviewer said

**Direction: visibility, not workflow.**
- "You're trying to basically create a workflow tool here and manage the signatures. I think this is maybe trying too much… then you would have to do all the audit trail."
- "People will not actually make the final approvals here. They will make it somewhere else. This is just giving the overall view of what's not matching."
- "Simplify, simplify, simplify." "Right now it feels like too much work to manage this… it should be more for visibility. What are you handing over?"
- Use: a snapshot at or after signature so the COO, Finance and Product know "what might come up… what's a little bit outside of the box or not clearly specified or what's been promised". After handover the work moves to project-management tools.

**What they liked.** "Commitments with open findings… that's perfect." The document list ("that's really good"). "Must fix before signing would be perfect, that's very clear."

**What they want on opening a deal.**
- A sales-stage bar at the top (discovery → proposal → negotiation → signed), with signed-but-open-issues called out.
- The documents that exist (or links to them).
- A short summary of the deal and its key deliverables.
- The open items, and the ability to add open items and deal-level notes themselves.

**Friction.**
- Too many clicks to accept a low-importance item; wants to decide from the main screen, maybe a "low impact" option.
- Should not have to retype a name when logged in.
- Evidence should not be needed for every item; a note with who and when is enough for audit.
- "Business impact: not assessed" read as wrong ("it is assessed").
- Coral Pay: Azure Australia East is not offered, yet Contract showed "No gap found"; confusing.
- UI "a little bit clunky… old-fashioned"; suggested getting modern design ideas.
- At 500 deals: search.

## What went wrong on our side

- Asked "this is all agentic, right?", the answer given described v1 ("rules decide because the agent didn't behave"). In v2 the model finds and code checks. Correct this in every future telling.
- The demo departed from the script (a decision recorded on the approval before the SOW step; reason "okay").
- None of the prepared questions to the reviewer were asked (who puts documents in, cost of an escalation, clean promises).

## Tension to resolve

The reviewer wants a visibility snapshot. The product's distinctive, tested part is checking that a fix actually closed a gap. Without it, the snapshot is close to what one model prompt produces (the single prompt also found 8/8 on Atlas Remit). One reviewer, a COO, not a Delivery lead.
